# Liquidity 50%

The liquidity allocation does two jobs: it is the tokens sold in the presale, and it is the tokens that sit in the trading pool next to the money the presale raised.

### Presale — 350,000,000 $MTA in three rounds

The presale runs for one month on Meteora's Presale Vault, or ends early the moment the final round fills. Each round has a fixed price, a fixed number of tokens, and a fixed per‑wallet limit. Earlier buyers pay less; later buyers pay more.

The presale runs for one month on Meteora's Presale Vault, or ends early the moment the final round fills. Each round has a fixed price, a fixed number of tokens, and a fixed per‑buyer limit. Earlier buyers pay less; later buyers pay more.

* Pay in USDC or SOL. The presale contract itself takes USDC, so every limit is an exact dollar amount; SOL is swapped to USDC at checkout in the same flow.
* Limits are $500 / $3,000 / $6,500 per round — $10,000 in total for any one buyer across all three. Round 1's small limit spreads its 100M tokens across at least 200 buyers.
* A round closes the instant it fills; the next round opens immediately.
* Deposits are final. There are no withdrawals or refunds once a purchase is made.
* Limits are per _buyer_, not per wallet. Wallets that share a funding source or move funds between each other are treated as one buyer and share one limit. This keeps the limits meaningful and the distribution wide.
* Tokens unsold at the end of the month are burned.

## Trading Pool — up to 150,000,000 $MTA

**100% of presale proceeds go into the trading pool.** On listing day, the USDC raised is paired with $MTA from this allocation on Meteora DAMM v2, and the liquidity position is locked permanently. Nobody — including the team — can remove it. The team's only claim on the pool is its trading fees, which fund operations and cash payouts to marketplace makers.

The number of tokens placed in the pool is set by the amount raised, so that the listing price always sits above the last presale round:

There is no separate exchange‑listing reserve: centralized‑exchange listings, if pursued, are funded from the pool's trading fees and the Treasury, and any tokens an exchange requires for market‑making are bought back from the open market rather than held out of supply.
